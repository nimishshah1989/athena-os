From the founder's 22-Jul-2026 walkthrough of the overhauled UI. These are durable taste laws, not one-off fixes:

1. **Explanation-or-delete.** Every element must have a clear purpose AND a visible explanation. A stat nobody can act on ("1 agent alert across 1 agent", non-clickable "uncalled capital") is clutter — explain it, make it actionable, or remove it.
2. **Click-through-to-source (no dead ends).** A claim like "3 commitments overdue" must deep-link to those exact commitments. Any aggregate → its constituents in one click.
3. **Arranged density, not cards.** Big padded cards read as waste. He wants "tabular" = systematically arranged rows (not literal tables), color-coded severity (e.g. months-overdue ramp), right-aligned amounts.
4. **One capture rail.** Global "Add note" (top-right) is THE capture point; inline Save-note/Voice-note blocks on pages like /today are duplicates — remove them. Attribution routes notes correctly anyway.
5. **Light, professional surfaces.** Dark-background panels (wealth appendix) read unprofessional. White/light backgrounds; he has a reference UI/UX website he intends to share (asked him for it 22-Jul).
6. **Visual over list for Journey.** He expected a wealth-growth-over-time visual with events on it, not a text list.
7. **Symmetric layout.** Balance-sheet boxes of uneven size (liability strip vs held-away box) feel unsystematic — same-grid, assets/liabilities/net.
8. **Self-serve onboarding.** Wants an in-tool "how the whole thing works" guide (bullets, flowcharts, ideal advisor flow) so no human onboarding is needed.
9. **No mystery buttons.** A "Save" button whose object is unclear (status editor) should auto-save; header actions (Meeting, Review pack) need visible intent.

**Why:** he is the buyer-proxy for non-technical wealth advisors; if HE can't parse a surface in seconds, the pilot fails.
**How to apply:** run every new surface against 1–3 and 9 before shipping; prefer removing over explaining when purpose is thin.
